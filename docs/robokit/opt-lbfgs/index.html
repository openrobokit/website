LBFGSOptimizer

Implements batched L-BFGS on manifold-valued variables. Source: src/robokit/opt/lbfgs_optimizer.py.

Overview

For the variable $\mathbf{x}$, each iteration computes the cost $c(\mathbf{x})$ and gradient $\mathbf{g}=\nabla c(\mathbf{x})$. $\mathbf{g}$ and the history give a descent direction $\mathbf{d}$. With no history, $\mathbf{d}$ follows $-\mathbf{g}$. A line search picks a step size $\alpha^*$, and $\mathbf{x}$ moves by $\alpha^*\mathbf{d}$.

flowchart TB I[Initial variable x] --> E["Cost c(x) and gradient g"] E --> D["Descent direction d<br/><i>computed using g and history</i>"] D --> L["Line-search step size α*"] L --> U[Update x] U -- Next iteration --> E

Descent direction

The direction $\mathbf{d}$ comes from the gradient $\mathbf{g}$ and a short history of steps. Iteration $t$, from $\mathbf{x}_t$ to $\mathbf{x}_{t+1}$, adds one history pair:

  • $\mathbf{s}_t = \alpha_t^* \mathbf{d}_t$: the tangent step applied to $\mathbf{x}_t$.
  • $\mathbf{y}_t = \mathbf{g}_{t+1} - \mathbf{g}_t$: the gradient change from $\mathbf{s}_t$. To first order,

$$\mathbf{g}(\mathbf{x}_t+\mathbf{s}_t) \approx \mathbf{g}(\mathbf{x}_t)+J_{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{x}_t)\mathbf{s}_t.$$

The gradient Jacobian is the cost Hessian, $J_{\mathbf{g}}(\mathbf{x})=H(\mathbf{x})=\nabla^2 c(\mathbf{x})$, so $\mathbf{y}_t\approx H(\mathbf{x}_t)\mathbf{s}_t$.

Newton's method uses $\mathbf{d}=-H(\mathbf{x})^{-1}\mathbf{g}$. L-BFGS replaces the exact inverse with an approximation $A$:

$$A\approx H(\mathbf{x})^{-1}, \qquad \mathbf{d}=-A\mathbf{g}.$$

Below, $i$ indexes the history pairs, oldest first. $A_i$ is the approximation after $i$ pairs, with $A_0=\gamma I$. BFGS adds pair $i$ by

$$A_{i+1}=V_iA_iV_i^\top+\rho_i \mathbf{s}_i \mathbf{s}_i^\top, \qquad V_i=I-\rho_i \mathbf{s}_i \mathbf{y}_i^\top, \qquad \rho_i=\frac{1}{\mathbf{y}_i^\top \mathbf{s}_i}.$$

A pair is used only when $\mathbf{y}_i^\top\mathbf{s}_i>0$; otherwise it is dropped. The update enforces $A_{i+1}\mathbf{y}_i=\mathbf{s}_i$ and keeps $A_{i+1}$ symmetric positive definite.

Why does the update work?
  1. Why does it enforce $A_{i+1}\mathbf{y}_i=\mathbf{s}_i$?

Since $\rho_i(\mathbf{y}_i^\top \mathbf{s}_i)=1$, $V_i^\top \mathbf{y}_i=\mathbf{y}_i-\rho_i\mathbf{y}_i (\mathbf{y}_i^\top \mathbf{s}_i)=\mathbf{0}$. Substituting into the update gives

$$A_{i+1}\mathbf{y}_i =V_iA_iV_i^\top \mathbf{y}_i +\rho_i\mathbf{s}_i(\mathbf{y}_i^\top \mathbf{s}_i) =\mathbf{0}+\rho_i\mathbf{s}_i(\mathbf{y}_i^\top \mathbf{s}_i) =\mathbf{s}_i.$$

  1. Why does it stay symmetric positive definite?

Let $A_i$ be symmetric positive definite. Then $V_iA_iV_i^\top$ and $\rho_i\mathbf{s}_i\mathbf{s}_i^\top$ are symmetric. If $\mathbf{y}_i^\top\mathbf{s}_i>0$, then $\rho_i>0$, and for any nonzero $\mathbf{z}$,

$$\mathbf{z}^\top A_{i+1}\mathbf{z} =(V_i^\top \mathbf{z})^\top A_i(V_i^\top \mathbf{z}) +\rho_i(\mathbf{s}_i^\top \mathbf{z})^2>0.$$

So $A_{i+1}$ stays symmetric positive definite.

After all pairs, $A$ approximates $H(\mathbf{x})^{-1}$ and gives $\mathbf{d}=-A\mathbf{g}$. L-BFGS never builds the $n\times n$ matrix $A$. A two-loop recursion over $m$ history pairs computes $A\mathbf{g}$ in $O(mn)$ memory and $O(mn)$ operations, where $m\ll n$.

Normalization. By default, normalize_direction=False keeps the direction's length. Set it to True to scale the direction to unit length before the line search.

How does the matrix update become two loops?

The direction needs $A_m\mathbf{g}$, where $m$ is the number of history pairs. Expanding $A_m\mathbf{g}$ with the BFGS update gives

$$\begin{aligned} A_m\mathbf{g} &=V_{m-1}A_{m-1}(V_{m-1}^\top\mathbf{g}) +\rho_{m-1}\mathbf{s}_{m-1} (\mathbf{s}_{m-1}^\top\mathbf{g}) \\ &=V_{m-1}\Big[ V_{m-2}A_{m-2}(V_{m-2}^\top V_{m-1}^\top\mathbf{g}) +\rho_{m-2}\mathbf{s}_{m-2} (\mathbf{s}_{m-2}^\top V_{m-1}^\top\mathbf{g}) \Big] \\ &\quad+\rho_{m-1}\mathbf{s}_{m-1} (\mathbf{s}_{m-1}^\top\mathbf{g}) \\ &=\cdots \\ &=V_{m-1}\cdots V_0A_0 (V_0^\top\cdots V_{m-1}^\top\mathbf{g}) \\ &\quad+\cdots+\rho_{m-1}\mathbf{s}_{m-1} (\mathbf{s}_{m-1}^\top\mathbf{g}). \end{aligned}$$

The first loop computes the product $V_0^\top\cdots V_{m-1}^\top\mathbf{g}$. Define $\mathbf{q}_0=V_0^\top\cdots V_{m-1}^\top\mathbf{g}$. Each step goes from $\mathbf{q}_{i+1}$ to $\mathbf{q}_i$:

$$\mathbf{q}_i =V_i^\top\mathbf{q}_{i+1} =\mathbf{q}_{i+1} -\rho_i\mathbf{y}_i(\mathbf{s}_i^\top\mathbf{q}_{i+1}).$$

Starting from $\mathbf{q}_m=\mathbf{g}$, the loop runs backward from $i=m-1$ to $i=0$ and yields $\mathbf{q}_0$.

The second loop evaluates the remaining terms, starting from $A_0\mathbf{q}_0$. Define $\mathbf{r}_0=A_0\mathbf{q}_0=\gamma\mathbf{q}_0$. Each step goes from $\mathbf{r}_i$ to $\mathbf{r}_{i+1}$:

$$\mathbf{r}_{i+1} =V_i\mathbf{r}_i +\rho_i\mathbf{s}_i(\mathbf{s}_i^\top\mathbf{q}_{i+1}) =\mathbf{r}_i+\rho_i\mathbf{s}_i (\mathbf{s}_i^\top\mathbf{q}_{i+1}-\mathbf{y}_i^\top\mathbf{r}_i).$$

Starting from $\mathbf{r}_0$, the loop runs forward from $i=0$ to $i=m-1$ and yields $\mathbf{r}_m=A_m\mathbf{g}$.

Pseudocode:

$$\begin{array}{l} \textbf{input}: \mathbf{g},\ \text{history},\ \gamma \\ \textbf{initialize}: \mathbf{q}_m\leftarrow\mathbf{g} \\ \textbf{for }i=m-1,\ldots,0 \\ \hspace{5mm} \mathbf{q}_i\leftarrow\mathbf{q}_{i+1} -\rho_i\mathbf{y}_i(\mathbf{s}_i^\top\mathbf{q}_{i+1}) \\ \mathbf{r}_0\leftarrow\gamma\mathbf{q}_0 \\ \textbf{for }i=0,\ldots,m-1 \\ \hspace{5mm} \mathbf{r}_{i+1}\leftarrow\mathbf{r}_i +\rho_i\mathbf{s}_i (\mathbf{s}_i^\top\mathbf{q}_{i+1}-\mathbf{y}_i^\top\mathbf{r}_i) \\ \textbf{return}: \mathbf{d}\leftarrow-\mathbf{r}_m \end{array}$$

Initial scale. The start is $A_0=\gamma I$, with $\gamma$ fixed or adaptive. Barzilai-Borwein scaling picks $\gamma$ from the newest pair $(\mathbf{s}_{m-1},\mathbf{y}_{m-1})$, so $\gamma\mathbf{y}_{m-1}$ best fits $\mathbf{s}_{m-1}$:

$$\gamma =\frac{\mathbf{s}_{m-1}^\top\mathbf{y}_{m-1}} {\mathbf{y}_{m-1}^\top\mathbf{y}_{m-1}}.$$

Line search

The line search tries a fixed set of step sizes $\alpha_j$ at once and picks

$$\alpha^* = \begin{cases} \displaystyle \max_j\left\{ \alpha_j \;\middle|\; c(\mathbf{x}\oplus\alpha_j\mathbf{d}) \le c(\mathbf{x})+c_1\alpha_j\mathbf{g}^\top\mathbf{d} \right\}, & \text{if any candidate passes}, \\[3mm] 0, & \text{otherwise}. \end{cases}$$

Since $A$ is positive definite, $\mathbf{g}^\top A\mathbf{g}>0, \mathbf{g}^\top\mathbf{d}<0$. The Armijo condition $c(\mathbf{x}\oplus\alpha_j\mathbf{d})\le c(\mathbf{x})+c_1\alpha_j\mathbf{g}^\top\mathbf{d}$ requires the actual decrease to be at least $c_1$ of the predicted first-order decrease. $c_1$ defaults to $10^{-4}$; a larger value requires more decrease.
