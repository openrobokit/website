LMOptimizer

Implements batched Levenberg-Marquardt on manifold-valued variables. Source: src/robokit/opt/lm_optimizer.py.

Overview

For the variable $\mathbf{x}$, each iteration computes the residual vector $\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x})$, the cost $c(\mathbf{x})=\frac12\|\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x})\|^2$ and the residual Jacobian $J$. These give a damped step $\boldsymbol{\delta}$ and a candidate $\widehat{\mathbf{x}}=\mathbf{x}\oplus\boldsymbol{\delta}$. The step comes from a model of the cost near $\mathbf{x}$, so LM compares the actual cost drop with the predicted one. The comparison decides whether $\widehat{\mathbf{x}}$ replaces $\mathbf{x}$ and whether the damping $\lambda$ grows or shrinks.

flowchart TB X[Variable x] --> E["Compute residual r, cost c, and Jacobian J"] E --> S["Compute damped step δ"] S --> C["Evaluate candidate x ⊕ δ and its cost"] C --> A["Compare actual vs predicted drop<br/><i>keep or discard candidate, update λ</i>"] A -- Next iteration --> E

LM step

Gauss-Newton. For the cost $c(\mathbf{x})=\frac12\|\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x})\|^2$ and a tangent step $\boldsymbol{\delta}$, to first order $\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x}\oplus\boldsymbol{\delta})\approx \mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x})+J\boldsymbol{\delta}$.

This gives the subproblem $\min_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\; \frac12\|\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x})+J\boldsymbol{\delta}\|^2$, with gradient $J^\top(\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x})+J\boldsymbol{\delta})$. Setting it to zero gives the normal equations

$$J^\top J\boldsymbol{\delta}=-J^\top\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x}).$$

Solving it for $\boldsymbol{\delta}$ gives the candidate $\widehat{\mathbf{x}}= \mathbf{x}\oplus\boldsymbol{\delta}$. This is a Newton-like step: $J^\top\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x})=\nabla c(\mathbf{x})$ and $J^\top J\approx H(\mathbf{x})=\nabla^2c(\mathbf{x})$.

Levenberg-Marquardt. LM adds a penalty for large steps:

$$\min_{\boldsymbol{\delta}}\quad \frac12\left\|\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x})+J\boldsymbol{\delta}\right\|^2 +\frac{\lambda}{2}\left\|\boldsymbol{\delta}\right\|^2.$$

This gives

$$\left(J^\top J+\lambda I\right)\boldsymbol{\delta} =-J^\top\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x}).$$

A larger $\lambda$ gives a smaller step. The default linear_solver="cholesky" solves this system exactly. "cg" uses matrix-free conjugate gradient and stops at a tolerance or an iteration cap.

linear_solver="torch_cholesky" factors the dense normal equations with PyTorch in float64. It needs PyTorch and runs eagerly. Use it for wide coupled systems where factorization dominates runtime.

Trust region

At the candidate $\widehat{\mathbf{x}}$, the actual reduction is $c(\mathbf{x})-c(\widehat{\mathbf{x}})$. The Gauss-Newton model predicts

$$\begin{aligned} \Delta c_{\mathrm{pred}} &=\frac12\|\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x})\|^2 -\frac12\|\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x})+J\boldsymbol{\delta}\|^2 \\ &=-\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x})^\top J\boldsymbol{\delta} -\frac12(J\boldsymbol{\delta})^\top(J\boldsymbol{\delta}). \end{aligned}$$

For $\Delta c_{\mathrm{pred}}>0$, the gain ratio compares the two:

$$\rho=\frac{c(\mathbf{x})-c(\widehat{\mathbf{x}})} {\Delta c_{\mathrm{pred}}+\epsilon}.$$

$\epsilon\ge0$ is a small stabilizer, $10^{-8}$ by default; $0$ gives the exact ratio. A $\rho$ near $1$ means the model was accurate. LM accepts the candidate exactly when

$$\boxed{\text{accept}\iff \Delta c_{\mathrm{pred}}>0\ \land\ \rho\ge\rho_{\min}}.$$

The default is $\rho_{\min}=10^{-3}$. With damping factor $\nu>1$, acceptance keeps $\widehat{\mathbf{x}}$ and sets $\lambda\leftarrow\lambda/\nu$ for a larger next step. Rejection keeps $\mathbf{x}$ and sets $\lambda\leftarrow\lambda\nu$ for a smaller one.

Algorithm

$$\begin{array}{l} \textbf{input}: \mathbf{x}_0,\quad \lambda_0,\quad \nu>1 \text{ (damping update factor)},\quad \rho_{\min} \text{ (acceptance threshold)}, \\ \hspace{13mm} \epsilon \text{ (numerical stabilizer)},\quad [\lambda_{\min},\lambda_{\max}],\quad T \text{ (iterations)} \\[1mm] \textbf{initialize}: \mathbf{x}\leftarrow\mathbf{x}_0,\ \lambda\leftarrow\lambda_0 \\[1mm] \textbf{for}\ t=1,\dots,T\ \textbf{do} \\ \hspace{5mm} \mathbf{r}\leftarrow\mathbf{r}(\mathbf{x}),\quad J\leftarrow J(\mathbf{x}),\quad c\leftarrow\tfrac12\|\mathbf{r}\|^2 \\ \hspace{5mm} \text{solve } (J^\top J+\lambda I)\boldsymbol{\delta}=-J^\top\mathbf{r} \quad \text{for } \boldsymbol{\delta} \\ \hspace{5mm} \widehat{\mathbf{x}}\leftarrow\mathbf{x}\oplus\boldsymbol{\delta}, \quad \text{evaluate }c(\widehat{\mathbf{x}})=\tfrac12\|\mathbf{r}(\widehat{\mathbf{x}})\|^2 \\ \hspace{5mm} \Delta c_{\mathrm{pred}}\leftarrow \tfrac12\|\mathbf{r}\|^2-\tfrac12\|\mathbf{r}+J\boldsymbol{\delta}\|^2 \\ \hspace{5mm} \textbf{if}\ \Delta c_{\mathrm{pred}}>0\quad\textbf{and}\quad \dfrac{c-c(\widehat{\mathbf{x}})}{\Delta c_{\mathrm{pred}}+\epsilon}\ge\rho_{\min} \\ \hspace{10mm} \mathbf{x}\leftarrow\widehat{\mathbf{x}},\quad \lambda\leftarrow\lambda/\nu \\ \hspace{5mm} \textbf{else} \\ \hspace{10mm} \lambda\leftarrow\lambda\nu \quad \text{($\mathbf{x}$ stays unchanged)} \\ \hspace{5mm} \lambda\leftarrow \operatorname{clip}(\lambda,\lambda_{\min},\lambda_{\max}) \\ \textbf{return}\ \mathbf{x} \end{array}$$
