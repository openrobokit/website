Grasp Optimization Algorithms: warp_gd and warp_lm

How the two grasp-optimizer backends work and when to use which. For running the stage see the grasp_optim tutorial; for every parameter see the grasp optimization API.

The problem

Grasp synthesis is energy minimization over a batch of grasps. Each grasp optimizes

  • the robot configuration q: hand joints, plus arm joints (fixed base) or a free SE(3) base pose (floating hand), and
  • a discrete contact assignment: which num_contact_points of the robot's predefined contact candidates are active.

The energy is a weighted sum of residual terms (robokit terms.dense.*):

TermPulls toward
DistanceTaskactive contact points on the object surface
ForceClosureTaskcontact normals that balance external wrenches (stability)
SceneCollisionTaskhand collision spheres out of the scene (object + table)
SelfCollisionTasklinks out of each other
PositionLimitjoints inside their limits
FingertipDistanceTask (optional)fingertip spheres onto the surface
HandUpAngleTask (optional)hand-up axis at least angle_z_min from world +z

Both backends share this formulation, the init samplers, and the Warp scene geometry (SDF volumes / mesh BVHs). The energy is highly non-convex and the contact assignment is discrete, so plain gradient descent gets stuck; each backend has its own escape mechanism.

warp_gd: simulated-annealing gradient descent

WarpGraspOptimizer (warp_optimizer.py, backend warp_gd) runs each grasp as an independent annealing chain for num_iters (~10k) iterations, DexGraspNet-style. One iteration:

  1. RMSprop step on the continuous variables; step size decays over time, joints are clamped to limits.
  2. With probability switch_possibility, propose a re-drawn contact set.
  3. Evaluate the proposal's energy and gradient.
  4. Metropolis accept: lower energy always wins; worse by d is accepted with probability exp(-d / T), where the temperature T cools over time. Rejected grasps roll back.

High T early lets grasps explore pose and contact assignment jointly; as T cools the chain settles into a low-energy basin. The whole iteration is captured as one CUDA graph, so the long loop has almost no launch overhead. Works for floating and fixed-base robots alike, which makes it the default backend.

warp_lm: multi-seed Levenberg-Marquardt

WarpGraspOptimizer (backend warp_lm) treats the same energy as nonlinear least squares and applies robokit's LMOptimizer: curvature-aware steps converge in tens of iterations instead of thousands. A single LM run is purely local, so exploration comes from:

  • Multi-seed: every init grasp spawns 24 jittered copies solved in parallel; the lowest-energy seed wins.
  • Contact resampling: LM cannot differentiate the discrete contacts, so every 8 iterations each candidate is joined by a copy with a fresh random contact set; both take the next LM steps, and the one with the lower energy stays.

warp_lm supports floating bases only. To target an arm+hand robot, set enable_ik_scoring=True: inits are pre-filtered by arm-IK reachability against the fixed-base robot and final seeds are ranked by energy + reachability penalty, so the grasps GraspTrajBuilder later IK-converts onto the arm are actually reachable.

Choosing a backend

warp_gdwarp_lm
MethodRMSprop + simulated annealingmulti-seed Levenberg-Marquardt
ExplorationMetropolis accept over pose + contacts24 seeds + periodic contact resampling
Iterations~10000 first-order steps64 curvature-aware steps
Speedbaselinetypically 2-3x faster
Robotsfloating and fixed basefloating base only (enable_ik_scoring bridges to arms)

Rule of thumb: warp_gd for fixed-base robots or when maximum exploration matters; warp_lm for floating-hand throughput, with enable_ik_scoring if the grasps will be converted to an arm. Both are compared in scripts/benchmark/grasp_opt/bench_grasp_optim.py.
